Parlett’s Primary Mechanisms

According to David Parlett's The Penguin Book of Card Games, card games are primarily classified by their underlying mechanics.

Trick-taking games

Plain-trick games: The primary objective is to win as many tricks as possible (e.g., Whist, Spades).

Point-trick games: The objective is to win tricks containing specific point-scoring cards (e.g., Skat, Pinochle).

Non-trick games

Card-taking games: Capturing cards from a central layout, often by matching or summing (e.g., Cassino).

Adding-up games: Playing cards to a common total without exceeding a limit (e.g., Cribbage, Blackjack).

Shedding games: The goal is to be the first to rid your hand of all cards (e.g., Crazy Eights, President).

Collecting games: Forming matched sets or sequences in your hand (e.g., Rummy, Piquet).

Ordering games (Patiences): Arranging a shuffled pack into a specific sequence or order (e.g., Solitaire).

Vying games: Betting on the strength of your hand or bluffing opponents (e.g., Poker, Brag).

Banking games: Players bet against a central "bank" or dealer (e.g., Pontoon, Baccarat).

Game Codex Report

1. Spades

Preliminaries: Four players play crosswise in fixed partnerships, using a standard 52-card pack ranking A, K, Q, J, T, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2. Thirteen cards are dealt to each player in ones. Spades are always trumps.

Object: Each partnership contracts to win a certain minimum number of tricks, or a player may bid "Nil" to lose every trick.

The Play: To the first trick, everyone must play their lowest club, with the highest club winning the trick and the lead. Players must follow suit if possible, otherwise they may play any card. Trumps (spades) may not be led until the suit is "broken" by a spade being played to a trick when a player cannot follow suit.

Scoring: A side taking at least as many tricks as bid scores 10 times its bid, plus 1 for each overtrick. An accumulation of 10 overtricks deducts 100 points (sandbagging). A failed contract loses 10 points per trick bid. A successful Nil bid scores 50 points, and a failed Nil bid loses 50. Game is 500 points.

2. Ninety-Nine

Preliminaries: Best for three players using a 36-card pack ranking A, K, Q, J, T, 9, 8, 7, 6. Twelve cards are dealt to each player.

Object: To bid and win an exact number of tricks, bidding by discarding three cards face down whose suits represent numbers (Clubs=3, Hearts=2, Spades=1, Diamonds=0).

The Play: The first deal is played at no trump; subsequent trumps are determined by the number of players who succeeded in the previous deal. Eldest leads, players follow suit if possible. The trick is taken by the highest card of the suit led or highest trump.

Scoring: Every player scores 1 point per trick won. Successful bidders score a bonus: 10 if all three succeeded, 20 if two, 30 if one. Premium bonuses are awarded for declaring or revealing bids before play. Game is 100 points.

3. Crazy Eights

Preliminaries: Two to seven players use a 52-card pack. Deal five cards each (seven if two players), stack the rest face down, and turn the top card to start the discard pile.

Object: To be the first to shed all one's cards.

The Play: Eldest plays a card matching the previous card by rank or suit. Eights are wild and can be played at any time to nominate a new suit to be followed. Anyone unable to follow must draw from the stock until they can play.

Scoring: The player who goes out collects the face value of all cards remaining in opponents' hands: Eights (50), courts (10), others at face value.

4. War (Battle)

Preliminaries: Deal all cards face down evenly among the players.

Object: To capture all the cards.

The Play: Players hold cards face down and simultaneously play their top card face up. The highest card wins the trick regardless of suit. Ties stay down and are won by the winner of the next untied round.

Scoring: The game is won by capturing all 52 cards.

5. Pontoon (Blackjack)

Preliminaries: Any number can play using one or more 52-card packs. banker deals one card face down to each player.

Object: To end up with cards totalling more than the banker's without exceeding 21. Ace counts as 1 or 11, face cards 10.

The Play: Players stake chips on their first card, then a second is dealt. Players may "buy" cards (staking more, face down), "twist" (face up for free), or stick. The banker then turns their cards and draws.

Scoring: A "pontoon" (Ace + 10-card) pays double. The banker wins all ties.

6. Poker (Draw Poker)

Preliminaries: Two to eight players use a 52-card pack. Players ante one chip. Deal five cards each.

Object: To win the pot by holding the best 5-card combination or by bluffing.

The Play: Players open, pass, call, raise, or fold. Active players then draw up to three replacement cards. A second betting interval follows.

Scoring: Highest-ranking Poker hand wins the pot.

7. Hearts

Preliminaries: Three to six players use a 52-card pack. Deal 13 cards each. Players pass three cards to a neighbor before play.

Object: To avoid winning tricks containing hearts or the Queen of Spades.

The Play: The holder of the 2 of Clubs leads first. Players must follow suit if possible. Hearts cannot be led until the suit is broken.

Scoring: 1 penalty point per heart, 13 for the Queen of Spades. "Hitting the moon" (all 14) deducts 26 from your total.

8. Solitaire (Klondike)

Preliminaries: A single player uses a shuffled pack.

Object: To put the shuffled pack back into order.

The Play: Cards are dealt face up to a wastepile. Aces go to the centre and are built upwards in suit to the King. The wastepile is turned to form a new stock when it runs out.

Scoring: Won if the player completes the sorting within a limited number of redeals.

9. Nap (Napoleon)

Preliminaries: Three to seven players use a stripped pack. Deal five cards each.

Object: To bid and win a chosen number of tricks.

The Play: Bids run from two to Nap (five), Wellington, and Blucher. The highest bidder leads, establishing the trump suit. Players follow suit if possible.

Scoring: A successful bidder wins units from each opponent; if unsuccessful, they pay the same amounts.

10. Napoleon at St Helena

Note: Not detailed in provided sources. Typically an Ordering (Patience) game.

11. Spite and Malice

Preliminaries: Two players use two different packs. Deal 26 cards to each player's "riddance pile" (top card up) and 5 cards to hand.

Object: To be the first to play out all 26 cards from one's riddance pile.

The Play: Cards played to eight centre piles built from Ace to King. Players may play from their upcard, hand, or discard piles. A turn ends by discarding to personal discard piles.

Scoring: Winner scores 5 points plus 1 per card left in loser's riddance pile.

12. Spit

Preliminaries: Two players use a 52-card pack. Each deals 26 cards into five stock piles with top cards up, and holds 11 "spit" cards.

Object: To be the first to get rid of all cards.

The Play: Players play simultaneously. Move top stock cards to central spit piles if they are one rank higher or lower.

Scoring: First to empty stock cards slaps a spit pile; loser takes the larger pile.

13. Racing Demon (Nerts)

Preliminaries: Two or more players, each with a distinguishable 52-card pack. Deal 13 to an "off-pile" and four to work-piles.

Object: Be the first to play off all cards from the off-pile.

The Play: Play is simultaneous. Aces move to the center to build in suit to Kings. Work-piles built down in alternating colors.

Scoring: 1 point per card in center Ace-piles, -2 points per card left in off-pile.

14. Cheat (Bullshit)

Preliminaries: Deal all cards out.

Object: To be the first out of cards.

The Play: Each player discards 1–4 cards face down and declares a rank in sequence (Aces, 2s, 3s...). Others may call "Cheat!". If lying, player takes the pile; if truthful, challenger takes it.

Scoring: The winner is the first to rid their hand of cards.

15. Durak

Preliminaries: Two to six players use a 36-card pack. Deal six cards each, turn trump, stack rest.

Object: To avoid being the last player left with cards in hand (the durak).

The Play: Attacker plays a card; defender must beat it with a higher card of same suit or a trump. If defender fails, they pick up the cards.

Scoring: Last player left with cards is the loser.

16. Cassino

Preliminaries: Up to four players. Deal four cards each and four face up to the table.

Object: Capture table cards by pairing or summing (e.g., 9 captures a 4 and 5).

The Play: Players play one card to capture table cards, trail a card, or create a "build".

Scoring: Points for most cards (3), most spades (1), Big Cassino (2), Little Cassino (1), per Ace (1).

17. Newmarket

Preliminaries: Four to eight players use a 52-card pack and a layout of four extra Kings. Deal cards evenly plus one dead hand.

Object: To be the first out of cards in each deal.

The Play: Cards played face up in ascending sequence regardless of suit. Sequence stops if a card is in the dead hand.

Scoring: First player out wins the kitty and chips from opponents for remaining cards.

18. Barbu

Preliminaries: Four players, 52-card pack. 28 deals total. Deal 13 each.

Object: Score positive points and avoid negative penalty points based on the dealer's contract.

The Play: Dealer chooses from seven contracts (No hearts, No Queens, Trump, Domino, etc.).

Scoring: Negative points for penalties; positive for finish order in Domino or tricks in Trump.

19. Skat

Preliminaries: Three active players using a 32-card pack. Deal 10 cards each and two to the "skat".

Object: High bidder (soloist) must win at least 61 card-points in tricks.

The Play: Contract can be Suit, Grand, or Null. Soloist plays against the other two.

Scoring: Base value of contract multiplied by factors (tops held/missing).

20. Euchre

Preliminaries: Four players (two partnerships) using a 25-card pack (inc. Joker). Deal five cards each.

Object: For the makers to win at least three tricks.

The Play: Top trumps are Joker, Right Bower (trump Jack), and Left Bower.

Scoring: Makers score 1 for 3-4 tricks, 2 for all 5 (march), and 4 for a lone march. Opponents score 2 for a "euchre".

22. President (Scum/Arsehole)

Preliminaries: Four to seven players, 52-card pack (2 is high). Deal all cards.

Object: Play out all cards as soon as possible.

The Play: Climbing mechanism. Subsequent players must match the number of cards with a higher rank.

Scoring: Social ranks determine card exchanges in the next deal.

23. Piquet

Preliminaries: Two players, 32-card pack. Deal 12 each.

Object: Gain majority points for combinations and tricks.

The Play: Elder discards and draws; Younger follows. Declare combinations (Point, Sequences, Sets). Play 12 tricks at no trump.

Scoring: Bonuses for Pique (30) and Repique (60).

24. Aces Up

Note: Not detailed in sources. Typically a fast-paced Solitaire/Ordering game.

1. Aces Up
Preliminaries: A single player uses a standard deck, with one card dealt to each of four Columns
. A single Foundation is on the right of the Tableau, and a single Waste pile is below the Stock pile
.
Object: To end with all four Aces at the top of the Columns, one Ace per Column, and no other cards in the Columns
.
The Play: From the topmost cards of each Column, remove all but the highest ranking card of each suit to the Foundation
. Aces rank highest
. Up to five cards may be placed onto the Waste pile
. When play comes to a standstill, click the Stock pile to deal another row of four cards
. Empty spaces in a Column can be filled with any topmost card of another Column
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring (one point is scored for each card correctly placed on the Foundation)
.
2. Baker’s Dozen
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards to 13 Columns, each containing four cards (three face up, one face down)
. On the deal, Kings are automatically moved to the bottom of the Columns
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from Ace to King
.
The Play: Build Columns down, regardless of suit, with a maximum of 11 cards per Column
. Only the topmost card can be moved from one Column to another or to a Foundation
. Empty Columns cannot be filled
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
3. Beleaguered Castle
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards face up to eight Columns, each containing six cards
. All four Aces are automatically placed on the Foundations in the middle of the Tableau
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up from Ace to King, regardless of suit
.
The Play: Build Columns down, regardless of suit
. Only the topmost card may be moved from a Column to a Foundation or to another Column, up to a maximum of 13 cards per Column
. Empty Columns may only be filled with Kings
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
4. Bowling
Preliminaries: Up to four players can play, using 10 empty spaces organized in a grid representing bowling "pins" numbered 1 to 10
. Two Waste piles represent the two bowling balls thrown for each frame
.
Object: To score the highest possible bowling score by filling in as many "pins" as possible, targeting 230 or greater
.
The Play: Cards are dealt one at a time from Stock
. A card placed on a pin must be lower in rank than cards already placed on higher pin numbers, with Aces ranking lowest
. Cards of identical rank may be placed on the same pin slot
. Unplayable cards go to a Waste pile; three cards in a Waste pile constitute one ball "thrown"
.
Scoring: Standard bowling scoring rules apply, with Strikes scored if all 10 pins are filled before three cards hit the Waste pile, and Spares if filled between three and five cards
.
5. Bristol
Preliminaries: A single player deals 24 cards to eight Columns containing three cards each
. The remaining cards make up the Stock pile
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, regardless of suit, from Ace to King
.
The Play: Build Columns down, regardless of suit
. Cards are flipped from the Stock pile three at a time, one to each of three Waste piles
. Only the topmost card of a Waste pile or Column can be moved
. Empty Columns cannot be filled, but empty spaces in Waste piles can be filled from the Stock pile
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
6. Calculation
Preliminaries: A single player starts with the Ace, 2, 3, and 4 removed and placed next to the four Foundations as guides
. There are five blank Columns at the top of the Tableau
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, regardless of suit, according to a defined mathematical order for each Foundation
.
The Play: Stock cards are flipped one at a time and played either to a Foundation or onto one of the five Columns
. A flipped card can be played on any card in the Columns, but once placed, it can only be moved to a Foundation
. Only the topmost card of a Column can be played to a Foundation
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
7. Canfield
Preliminaries: A single player deals four Columns containing a single card each, and 10 cards to a Reserve pile
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from a randomly selected starting rank, wrapping from King to Ace as necessary
.
The Play: Build Columns down in alternating colors
. Any number of Packed Cards may be moved between Columns
. Stock cards are flipped three at a time onto a single Waste pile, and the topmost card can be played
. Empty Columns are automatically filled with cards from the Reserve pile
. There is no limit on redeals
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
8. Cribbage Square (or Cribbage Patience)
Preliminaries: A single player uses an empty 4x4 grid and a Stock pile
. (Alternatively, 16 cards are dealt first into the square, and the 17th card is the starter
).
Object: To score the highest possible Cribbage hand in each of the four rows and columns, aiming for at least 75 points (or 61 points depending on the rule set)
.
The Play: Flip cards one at a time from the Stock and fill in each space in the grid in the most advantageous place
. Once a card is placed, it cannot be moved
. The final card dealt is the "starter" for calculating the scores
.
Scoring: Normal cribbage scoring applies (pairs, runs, flushes, 15s), treating each line of four cards plus the starter as a five-card hand
.
9. Eagle Wing
Preliminaries: A single player deals eight Columns containing a single card each, and 14 cards to a Reserve pile
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from a randomly selected starting rank, wrapping from King to Ace as necessary
.
The Play: Build Columns down, in suit, wrapping as necessary, up to a maximum of three cards
. The topmost card or a set of Packed Cards may be moved
. The Stock pile is flipped one card at a time onto a Waste pile
. Empty Columns are filled automatically with cards from the Reserve pile
. Cards are redealt once
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
10. Eight Off
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards to eight Columns, four with seven cards and four with six cards
. There are eight Reserve piles at the bottom of the Tableau
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from Ace to King
.
The Play: Build Columns down and in suit
. The topmost card of any Column may be played to a Foundation, any Column, or any Reserve pile
. Any Reserve card may be played to a Foundation or Column
. Empty Columns may be filled with any topmost card from a Column or Reserve pile
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
11. Eliminator
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards face up into four Columns, 13 cards each
. There are eight Foundations on the right
.
Object: To remove all cards from the four Columns, using as few Foundations as possible
.
The Play: The topmost card of any Column can be moved to any Foundation
. Build Foundations up or down, regardless of suit, wrapping cards from King to Ace, or Ace to King as necessary
. Cards cannot be moved between Columns, and empty Columns cannot be filled
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: The player scores by using as few Foundations as possible
.
12. Flower Garden
Preliminaries: A single player deals 30 cards to six Columns, five cards each
. The remaining 22 cards make up the Reserve pile dealt face up at the bottom
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from Ace to King
.
The Play: Build Columns down, regardless of suit
. Only the topmost card of a Column can be played to a Foundation or another Column
. Any Reserve card can be played on a Foundation or Column, and empty Columns can be filled with any available card
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
13. Fortress
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards face up into 10 Columns
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from the rank of the first card played to a Foundation (which is selected by the player)
.
The Play: Build the Foundations up, in suit, wrapping King to Ace as necessary
. Build Columns up or down, regardless of suit, moving only the topmost card
. Empty Columns can be filled with any available card
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
14. Four Free (FreeCell)
Preliminaries: A single player deals the deck face up in eight columns
. There are four home Foundations and four free Foundations (reserve cells)
.
Object: To move all the cards to the home Foundations, stacking one for each suit in order of rank
.
The Play: Players move cards to build up the home Foundations, using the four free Foundations as temporary place holders
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
15. Gaps
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards face up in four rows
. All 2s are automatically placed at the left end of each row, and Aces are removed to form four gaps
.
Object: To arrange four rows of cards, one row of each suit, sequentially from 2 to King
.
The Play: Fill gaps with the next higher card of the same suit as the card on the left of the gap, or with the next lower card of the same suit as the card on the right of the gap
. Wrapping from King to Ace is not allowed
.
Scoring: One point is scored for each card positioned correctly
.
16. Golf
Preliminaries: A single player deals seven Columns, each containing five cards, on the Tableau
.
Object: To clear all cards from the Tableau onto a single Foundation
.
The Play: Choose any card from a Column to start the Foundation
. Build the Foundation pile up or down, regardless of suit, with Ace low and King high (no wrapping allowed)
. Only the topmost card of any Column can be played
. When stuck, flip one card from the Stock pile onto the Foundation
. Cards cannot be moved between Columns
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: The score is the number of cards left in the Columns; lower is better
.
17. Klondike
Preliminaries: A single player deals 28 cards to seven Columns, with only the topmost card face up
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from Ace to King
.
The Play: Build Columns down in alternating colors
. Packed Cards can be moved as a unit
. The topmost card of a Column can be played to a Foundation
. Stock cards are flipped three at a time to a Waste pile
. Empty Columns can only be filled with Kings
. There is no limit on redeals
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
18. La Belle Lucie
Preliminaries: A single player places all Aces on their Foundations, and deals all remaining cards face up in 16 Columns, each containing three cards
.
Object: To build the Foundations up, in suit, from Ace to King
.
The Play: Build Columns down, in suit, with no more than seven cards per Column
. Only the topmost card of a Column can be moved
. Empty Columns cannot be filled
. When stuck, gather all cards in the Columns, reshuffle, and redeal (allowed up to three times)
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
19. Nestor
Preliminaries: A single player deals six Columns, seven cards each
. The remaining 10 cards are laid face up as the Reserve
.
Object: To play all cards to the single Foundation
.
The Play: Play pairs of cards of the same rank to the Foundation in any sequence
. Only the topmost card of any Column, or any card in the Reserve, may be played
. Cards cannot be moved between Columns
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring (game won when all are paired)
.
20. Penguin
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards face up in seven Columns containing seven cards each
. The starting rank is randomly selected, three are placed on Foundations, and the fourth is randomly placed among the Columns
. There are seven Reserve piles
.
Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from the randomly selected starting rank, wrapping from King to Ace as necessary
.
The Play: Build Columns down, in suit, wrapping as necessary
. The topmost Column card can be played to a Foundation, another Column, or to a Reserve pile
. Packed cards can be moved as a unit
. Reserve cards can be played back to a Column or Foundation
. Empty Columns can be filled with any available card
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
21. Poker Square (or Poker Patience)
Preliminaries: A single player uses a 5x5 grid and all cards in the Stock pile
.
Object: To build the highest possible poker hand in each of the five rows and columns, scoring at least 100 points (or 75/200 depending on the rule set)
.
The Play: Flip cards one at a time from Stock and fill in each space in the grid
. Once placed, a card cannot be moved
.
Scoring: Treat each of the five rows and columns as a Poker hand, and score them according to standard combinations
.
22. Pyramid
Preliminaries: A single player deals 28 cards face up in a pyramid, and seven cards face up along the bottom as a Reserve
.
Object: To remove all cards off the Tableau by playing all pyramid and Reserve cards to a single Foundation
.
The Play: Move cards in pairs totaling 13 off the pyramid onto the Foundation
. Only Fully Exposed cards can be moved, and Kings move singly
. Any Reserve card can be used to make a pair
. Stock cards are flipped one at a time to a Waste pile
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
23. Scorpion
Preliminaries: A single player deals all except three cards into seven Columns of seven cards each (first four Columns have two cards face down)
. The remaining three cards go to the Stock pile
.
Object: To build four sets of cards down on the Tableau from King to Ace, in suit
.
The Play: Build Columns down, in suit
. Any face-up card can be played to a Fully Exposed card in another Column, and cards on top of it move with it
. When stuck, click the Stock pile to deal the final three cards to the first three Columns
. Empty Columns can be filled with any card or group
. There is no redeal
.
Scoring: One point is scored for every card put into a correct position
.
24. Seahaven Towers
Preliminaries: A single player deals the deck face up in 10 Columns of five cards each
. The two remaining cards are dealt to Reserve spaces
.
Object: To build up each suit from Ace to King on the Foundations
.
The Play: Only cards on the bottom of a Column or in a Reserve space can be moved
. Build Columns down, in suit
. Any card can be moved to a free Reserve space
. Empty Columns can be filled with Kings
.
Scoring: Traditional scoring
.
25. Shamrocks
Preliminaries: A single player deals all cards face up to 18 Columns (all but one contain three cards; the last has one)

Object: To build the four Foundations up, in suit, from a player-selected starting rank, wrapping from King to Ace as necessary

. Empty Columns are not filled

26. Slide
Preliminaries: A single player deals 24 cards face up in a 6x4 grid, with the rest making up the Stock pile
.
Object: To arrange cards in the grid so three cards of the same rank are aligned vertically, aiming for a score of 300 or greater
.
The Play: Cards are moved left or right by sliding them via arrows
. When three of the same rank align vertically, they are removed
. Empty spaces are filled directly from Stock or by sliding
. Cards that slide off the end go to a Waste pile, or back to the Stock if they match another card in the row
.
Scoring: The first three-of-a-kind set scores 1 point times the rank, the second scores 2 times the rank, up to 13 points times the rank

27. Spiderette
Preliminaries: A single player deals seven Columns (two cards face down, one face up) and leaves the rest in the Stock pile

28. Strategy
